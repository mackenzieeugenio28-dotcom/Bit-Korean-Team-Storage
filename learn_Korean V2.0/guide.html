The method

The four-step learning loop

1

1 · Listen & guess

Press play and listen to the romanised line. Try to guess the meaning from context and sound before checking. This trains your ear and makes the answer stick.

2

2 · Line breakdown

Read the original Hangul line and study each word’s meaning. Notice how particles connect the words.

3

3 · Words & grammar

Review the key vocabulary and read the one grammar point. Say the words out loud twice — speaking helps memory more than silent reading.

4

4 · Mini quiz

Answer three questions. Score 2 or more to complete the lesson, earn 10 XP and extend your streak.

Beginner’s guide

The Korean alphabet is called 한글 (Hangul). It was created in 1443 under King Sejong and published in 1446 as the Hunminjeongeum. There are only 24 basic letters — 14 consonants and 10 vowels — so you can learn them all in a few days.

The shapes are not random: consonants imitate the lips, tongue and throat as the sound is made (ㅁ looks like a closed mouth, ㄴ like the tongue on the roof of the mouth), and the vowels are built from three strokes standing for heaven, earth and human.

Tip: memorise the sounds of the 24 basic letters first — reading the lines on the lesson page gets much easier after that.

Korean is written in syllable blocks: an initial consonant, then a vowel, and a final consonant if needed — all stacked into one square, so 한 is ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ. Read left to right and top to bottom and those 24 letters turn into full lines of dialogue.

Chatting in Korean is a study method too

Watching lines and memorising words are input — a language only stays with you once you use it. Drop what you just learned into a real chat: greet a friend, send a line like “오늘 뭐 해?” (what are you up to today?), leave a Korean comment. Your brain holds on far better when it knows a real person is going to read it.

You do not have to wait until you are fluent. One sentence a day is enough — when you get stuck, come back to the matching lesson and its dialogue, then use it again next time. Being corrected once usually teaches you more than copying a phrase ten times.

Tip: add a Korean keyboard to your phone so you can type anywhere, and send one Korean sentence a day — a week is enough to feel the difference.

Gamification

How XP, levels and streaks work

XP points

Finish a lesson → +10 XP. XP is saved in your browser with localStorage.

Levels

Level = floor(XP ÷ 100) + 1. Level up every 100 XP.

Day streak

Study on two days in a row and the streak grows. Miss a day and it resets to zero.

Progress

Completed lessons are marked on the course page and shown on your home dashboard.

Study tips

Five habits that actually work

  • Do one lesson a day — five minutes beats one hour once a week.
  • Say every line out loud. Mimic the tone, not just the words.
  • Turn on Korean subtitles when you watch dramas, and pause to repeat lines.
  • Write each new word by hand once — handwriting strengthens memory.
  • Replay completed lessons after a week to move them into long-term memory.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to register?

No. You can learn immediately. An account (demo) only keeps a personalised login experience.

Where is my progress stored?

In your browser’s localStorage. It stays as long as you do not clear site data or browse in private mode.

Is there real audio?

This V1.0 prototype plays the romanised line instead of real audio. Audio files are on the roadmap.

How many lessons are there?

Nine lessons in V1.0 — a Hangul alphabet primer plus eight classic drama scenes. More are planned.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes — the layout is responsive and works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari.
